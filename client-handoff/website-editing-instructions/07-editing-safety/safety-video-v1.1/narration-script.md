## Scene 1 — Website editing safety and recovery

This lesson explains the safety rules that apply to every website edit. Make only the approved change, protect the live website, save one time, clear only the applicable cache, verify the exact public result, and stop before a small uncertainty becomes a larger problem.

## Scene 2 — Required WordPress access

Use the minimum role assigned to you. Editor is sufficient for routine approved content. Administrator controls, users, plugins, themes, settings, servers, payments, and store integrations require separate authorization. Never borrow another person's login simply to reach a menu.

## Scene 3 — Confirm the task before touching the editor

Before editing, confirm the live domain, the exact page or record, and the approved replacement information. If the request is missing a name, date, image, category, link, or final wording, stop before opening the editor and ask for the missing information.

## Scene 4 — Confirm the live domain and your own login

Use rebekahspureliving.com forward slash W P dash admin. Confirm the address begins with the live domain. Sign in with your own named account. Do not use an old staging address, a shared account, or another person's saved login.

## Scene 5 — Open the exact item named in the request

Choose the correct section, such as Practitioners, Events, Posts, or Pages. Match the exact title, name, store, and date before selecting Edit. If two records look similar or the identifying information does not match, stop and confirm the correct item.

## Scene 6 — Change only the approved fields

Leave unrelated copy, images, categories, links, dates, and settings unchanged. Do not redesign a page while making a content correction. Compare the old value with the approved replacement before typing, and reread the complete changed area when finished.

## Scene 7 — Keep new or uncertain work in Draft

Use Draft for new content until every required detail is complete and approved. Use Preview to inspect it privately. Do not use Publish merely to see how it might look. For an existing item, use the approved save action only after the change is reviewed.

## Scene 8 — Do not change design, plugins, or server controls

Routine editors should not change Appearance, Plugins, Settings, Cloudways, payment, store, integration, or optimization controls. Do not install, update, disable, reconnect, configure, or restart anything. Take a screenshot of the problem and contact Blue Nova.

## Scene 9 — Review images, links, dates, and public claims

Confirm images are approved and correctly cropped. Open every changed link. Recheck dates, times, prices, health wording, addresses, phone numbers, names, and store information. These details can cause customer confusion when a small typo is published.

## Scene 10 — Save once and wait for confirmation

Choose Save, Update, or Publish according to the approved task. Select the correct button one time and wait for WordPress confirmation. Do not click repeatedly, close the page while it is saving, or create a duplicate item to work around a delay.

## Scene 11 — Clear only the applicable cache

Start with the exact record or page cache when available. Use broader Breeze or Varnish purges only when approved. Do not clear unrelated caches and do not change plugin, cache, server, database, or optimization settings.

## Scene 12 — Verify publicly and stop if the result is wrong

Open the exact canonical public address with no extra text. Confirm the visible change and test affected controls at desktop and phone sizes. If the result is wrong, missing, or broken, stop. Do not keep saving or purging. Record what you see and contact Blue Nova.

## Scene 13 — Know when to stop and ask for help

Safe editing means opening the correct item, making only the approved change, saving once, clearing only the applicable cache, and verifying publicly. Stop whenever the task, screen, permission, save result, or public page does not match what these instructions describe.
