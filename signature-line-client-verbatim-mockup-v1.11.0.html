Rooted in Herbal Quality

Many Rebekah’s Private Label products begin with carefully sourced botanicals.

Our manufacturing supplier has developed long-standing relationships with organic farmers, growers and trained wild harvesters throughout the world—from New Mexico and Iowa to Egypt and Vanuatu. These relationships help provide access to fresh, recent-harvest botanicals from suppliers who share a commitment to plant quality and responsible sourcing.

Depending on the individual formula, botanicals may be organically grown, wild harvested or sourced from qualified suppliers.

Whole herbs may be evaluated and processed using methods appropriate to the plant and intended formula. Different extraction techniques may be used to draw out naturally occurring botanical constituents and create liquid extracts, capsules and other finished products.

Extensive Ingredient and Finished-Product Testing

Quality control begins before a product ever reaches the bottle.

The manufacturing facility tests incoming ingredients and also evaluates formulations and finished products before release. Testing is designed to help verify ingredient identity, purity, potency and stability throughout the manufacturing process.

Depending on the ingredient or formula, testing may include:

  • Identity testing
  • Potency or strength testing
  • Microbiological testing
  • Purity testing
  • Heavy-metal analysis
  • Pesticide testing
  • Allergen testing when applicable
  • Stability testing
  • Botanical identification
  • Visual, microscopic and sensory evaluation

The exact testing performed varies according to the ingredient, formula and applicable quality specifications.

Advanced Testing Meets Traditional Botanical Knowledge

Herbal supplements require more than attractive packaging. Botanical identity, harvesting, handling and extraction methods can all influence the quality of the finished product.

The manufacturing laboratory uses a combination of traditional botanical evaluation and modern analytical technology to evaluate raw materials and finished products.

Testing methods may include:

FTIR – Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy

Used to help identify chemical compounds and confirm ingredient identity.

GC – Gas Chromatography

Used to evaluate chemical profiles and potency and is commonly used in the analysis of essential oils.

HP-TLC – High Performance Thin Layer Chromatography

Helps identify botanical ingredients, including plant genus and species.

HPLC – High Performance Liquid Chromatography

Used to isolate and measure specific chemical markers and evaluate the potency of certain plant compounds, vitamins and other ingredients.

ICP-MS – Inductively Coupled Plasma Mass Spectrometry

Used to measure and quantify heavy metals in raw materials and finished products.

Microscopy

Examines the cellular structure of whole botanicals to assist with plant identification.

Macroscopy

Uses the visible characteristics of whole plants to help verify botanical genus and species.

Microbiological Testing

Evaluates raw materials and finished products for microorganisms such as aerobic bacteria, yeast, mold, E. coli, Staphylococcus and Salmonella.

Organoleptic Evaluation

Uses traditional botanical characteristics such as taste, color, aroma and appearance as additional quality markers.

Titration

May be used to measure the potency of certain vitamins and minerals.

UV-VIS – Ultraviolet-Visible Spectroscopy

Used to measure concentrations of specific compounds within certain botanical ingredients.

This combination of modern laboratory technology and traditional botanical knowledge gives our manufacturing partner multiple ways to evaluate the identity and quality of the ingredients used in our products.

Heavy-Metal and Pesticide Testing

Heavy-metal analysis is an important part of the quality-control process for applicable raw materials and finished products.

ICP-MS technology may be used to quantify metals present within an ingredient or formula.

Pesticide testing is also performed when applicable based on the botanical, ingredient and established quality specifications.

These additional safeguards are especially important to us because quality is about more than what is added to a product—it is also about carefully evaluating what should not be there.
